# The Generator

The order of operations I run when you say *"make an episode about X."* If you don't
name an X, I draw from the rule bank below.

---

## Order of operations

**Write Scene C first.** Always. The collision is the episode; everything else is
retrofitted to make it inevitable. Writing forwards produces a sketch with a weak
ending, every time.

1. **Pick the RULE.** From the bank, or from your prompt. It must be real, unwritten, and defensible — a stranger in a pub should nod at it. (QA 3 and 4 fail here more than anywhere else.)
2. **Pick the VICTIM.** Someone Sam applies the rule to, who *does not yet have* power over him. Preferably mild, low-status in the moment, and not obviously connected to the business.
3. **Pick the SEED.** A throwaway fact about the day's job — who's signing off, who's doing the walk-through, whose brother-in-law, when the stone is arriving. It must be sayable in under four seconds while walking away.
4. **Build the collision.** The victim *is* the seed. Now the mild man from Scene B controls the money, the sign-off, the template date, or the referral.
5. **Write Scene C.** Reveal, then the Snap: accusation → one beat of silence → Sam's technically-correct defence.
6. **Write the button.** Turn Sam's own rule or his own unit of measure back on him. This is the line people repeat.
7. **Write Scene A backwards from the seed.** Sam explains the rule to a friendly; the seed goes in the last line, thrown away.
8. **Write Scene B.** Sam wins the argument and loses the room. The victim's last line should be a single flat Australian word — *righto*, *no worries*, *fair enough* — that reads as surrender and is actually a sentence being passed.
9. **Cut to 135–165 words.** Cut adjectives, cut second examples, cut anything explained twice. Protect the silence after the accusation.
10. **Run the 25-point checklist** in `06-sixty-second-format.md`.
11. **Output:** shooting script with timecodes, shot list, continuity notes, prop tracking.

## The collision must be plausible, not coincidental

Melbourne trade is a small world, and that's the licence. Legitimate connections:

- The client's parent / in-law / neighbour doing the walk-through
- A subcontractor's new employee
- Someone from the same footy club, church, or school pickup
- A supplier's rep who covers the whole northern suburbs
- The building surveyor, the strata guy, the owner's builder
- Somebody's sister-in-law in a book club with the client

**Illegitimate:** pure chance in a city of five million. If the connection needs
explaining, it's the wrong connection — the reveal should land in a look, not a line.

## The rule bank

Thirty unwritten rules, all defensible, all Melbourne. Each one is an episode.

### On the road
1. You must wave when someone lets you merge — and there's a correct amount of hand.
2. How long you can park across a driveway for "just two minutes."
3. Whether a tradie's ute in a residential street is exempt from anything at all.
4. Whether you can take the toll road on a job and bill it.
5. Whether the person who indicates first owns the space.

### On site
6. A tradie uses the downstairs toilet, never the ensuite.
7. Whether "while you're here" is free, and for how many minutes.
8. Whether a fart in a client's house must be acknowledged.
9. Whether you can accept a coffee from a client and then not drink it.
10. Whether accepting the coffee obliges you to absorb a variation.
11. Whether you can play your own radio station in someone's house.
12. How many days late a subcontractor can be before you're allowed to mention it.
13. Whether you can eat your lunch in the client's finished kitchen.
14. Who owns the offcuts.

### In the workshop
15. Who gets the last of the milk, and whether buying milk creates a debt.
16. Whether the good chisel can be lent, and to whom.
17. Whether the apprentice pays for his own lunch.
18. Whether a birthday collection is genuinely voluntary.
19. Whether you can put your name on a tool with a paint pen.
20. Whether hi-vis is a costume if you don't have a job.

### Social
21. Whether onion goes above or below the sausage at a Bunnings sizzle.
22. Whether "how ya goin'" requires an actual answer.
23. Whether you can bring your own coffee into a café.
24. Whether you're obliged to look at someone's photos on their phone, and how many.
25. Whether you can leave a group chat.
26. Whether you must attend the funeral of a client's mother you never met.
27. Whether you can leave a wake before the food comes out.
28. Whether saying "no worries" obliges you to actually have no worries.
29. Whether a compliment about someone's partner requires reciprocation.
30. Whether you can decline to guess someone's footy team.

### Rules for adding to the bank
A rule qualifies if:
- **It's unwritten.** If you can look it up, it's a regulation, not a rule. (Exception: rules that *are* written down but that nobody has read — the onion directive — are the best of all, because Sam has read it.)
- **It's contested.** Two reasonable people can differ.
- **It's petty.** The stakes must be near-zero, so the consequences can be enormous.
- **It's enforceable socially.** Someone can be shamed for breaking it.

## Output format

Every generated episode ships as:

```
TITLE + logline (one sentence)
THREAD MAP (Thread 1, Thread 2, where planted, where paid)
SHOOTING SCRIPT (timecoded, scene by scene, with camera geometry marked)
SHOT LIST (per scene: setup, lens, framing, duration)
CONTINUITY NOTES (wardrobe, props, vehicle, tape measure location)
QA (checklist result, word count, runtime estimate)
```

See `episodes/` for three worked examples.
