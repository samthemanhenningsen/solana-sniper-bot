# *Melbourne Cabinet Makers with Enthusiasm* — Series Bible

Fixed reference. Everything here is locked so that clips shot months apart cut
together. Changing anything in this file is a series-level decision, not an episode
decision.

---

## Premise

A cabinet maker who runs a small custom joinery business out of a factory unit in
Thomastown believes there is a correct way to do everything, and that pointing it out
is a public service. The trade runs on referrals, favours, and never saying the
uncomfortable thing. He says it. Every episode, a rule he is technically right about
costs him a job.

The conceit is the Curb conceit: **the lead plays a heightened version of himself under
his own name.** Default is **Sam**; swap for whoever is actually performing.

## The lead

**SAM** — mid-40s. Co-owner. Calm, reasonable tone at all times, including at maximum
offence. Asks questions instead of making statements — it's how he gets people to state
a position he can then hold them to. Uses precise numbers. Never raises his voice.
**Never embarrassed** — baffled and indignant only.

- **Wardrobe (locked):** faded navy work polo, business logo on the left chest. Khaki work pants. Brown Blundstone 500s. Carpenter's pencil behind the right ear, always. Orange safety glasses pushed up on his forehead, always, including indoors and at dinner.
- **Vehicle:** white 2016 Toyota HiAce, roof rack, business name in vinyl on both sides. **The "K" in the business name is peeling.** It is never fixed. It gets worse across the series.
- **Tell:** he holds a folder. He is always holding a folder.

## The company

**HENNING & KOSTAS CUSTOM JOINERY** — established 2009. Six people. The vinyl on the van
reads HENNING & OSTAS.

## Regular cast

### DAVE "KOSTA" KOSTAS — *The Validator*
50s, Greek-Australian, Reservoir. Sam's business partner. Agrees with Sam completely in
private and abandons him instantly in public. Runs the numbers and the quotes. Has a
horror of being disliked, which is the engine of every betrayal.
- **Wardrobe:** same polo, untucked. Cargo shorts in all weather. Sunglasses pushed back on his head, indoors.
- **Function:** the theory scene. Sam explains the rule to Kosta in the van; Kosta agrees; Kosta will not back him up forty seconds later.

### VOULA KOSTAS — *The Consequence*
Dave's wife. Runs the office, the invoices, and the debtors. Arrives, detonates, leaves.
**Maximum 12 seconds of screen time per episode.** Never in the workshop longer than
that. Never has a change of heart, a reason, or a second scene.
- **Wardrobe:** large-print blouse, immaculate hair, gold. Holding a phone in one hand and a paper invoice in the other, simultaneously, always.
- **Function:** weather. Do not give her theories.

### BAZZA (BARRY NOLAN) — *The Escalator*
Sam's mate. Has been on Sam's couch since the divorce, duration unspecified and
increasing. No job. Total, unshakeable confidence. Gives tactically catastrophic advice
in the tone of a man stating the obvious, and Sam takes it, because it is delivered with
more certainty than anything else in his life.
- **Wardrobe:** a different fluoro hi-vis vest every episode, despite not having a job. Never explained.
- **Function:** gives Sam permission and a plan. The plan is always the worst available option.

### TREV — *The Mirror*
Stonemason. Supplies the benchtops. Hypochondriac and catastrophist. Every conversation
routes through his back, his gut, or the thing on his arm. His benchtops are
structurally always three days late, and he is never once at fault for it.
- **Function:** lets Sam be the reasonable one for twenty seconds, which the audience needs before Sam becomes unreasonable again.

### NIC — *The Reality Check*
Sam's partner. Landscape architect, Northcote. Warm, funny, patient, and visibly
depleting. She is the audience's proxy and the only character permitted to be simply
correct.
- **Function:** she asks Sam the question the audience is asking. She never gets a satisfying answer.

### JARRAH — *The Apprentice*
19. Second-year. Vapes. Speaks in *yeah nah*, *cooked*, *that's mad*. Has memorised the
entire Blum catalogue and is, on hardware, the most competent person in the business.
Sam finds this enraging and cannot say why.
- **Function:** the flat, accurate observation from the corner of the workshop, usually as the button.

## Recurring antagonists

One per episode. These are types, and they recur across the series with the same names
so the world stays coherent.

- **FIONA** — the Brighton client. A Pinterest board with a person attached. Specs the impossible, gently. Her threats are social, never financial: *"I've got a book club on Thursday."*
- **CRAIG** — the builder. Never leaves the right gap. Says *"she'll be right"* and means *"that's your problem now."* Genuinely likes Sam, which makes it worse.
- **DEAN** — of Dean's Custom Joinery. The rival. Undercuts on price, uses 16mm where 18mm is specified, and is beloved by everyone.
- **THE INSPECTOR** — parking. Never named. Appears at the worst moment. Entirely within his rights, which is unbearable.
- **THE TRADE COUNTER BLOKE** — at the timber yard. Is on the phone. Remains on the phone.

## Locations (locked — dress identically every shoot)

1. **THE WORKSHOP** — Thomastown. Tilt-slab factory unit, roller door half up. Panel saw, edgebander, MDF dust on everything, offcut rack. A radio on Triple M. A kettle, a jar of instant, and a milk situation. A whiteboard with job names in three colours of marker.
2. **THE VAN CAB** — the theory scene. Two-shot from the bonnet or the back seat, windscreen blown out. Coffee cups in the console. This is where rules are explained.
3. **PISTONE'S** — the coffee place, Brunswick. Two outdoor tables on the footpath, tram noise.
4. **THE CLIENT'S HOUSE** — Brighton. Mid-renovation. Plastic sheeting, no kitchen, a sink on the floor, one good chandelier already installed for some reason.
5. **THE TRADE COUNTER** — timber/hardware supplier. Fluorescent, a queue, a phone ringing.
6. **SAM'S HOUSE** — Coburg. Kitchen bench, back deck. Bazza on the couch in the background of everything.

## Running gags — plant early, pay across the series

- **The tape measure.** Sam's. It goes missing. It is always eventually found on someone else's belt, and they are always unbothered.
- **The peeling K.** HENNING & OSTAS. It degrades over the series. Nobody mentions it until it becomes plot.
- **"I'm a cabinet maker. Not a chippy."** Nobody in the world observes the distinction. Sam observes it constantly.
- **The 50% deposit.** Sacred to Sam. Negotiable to everyone else.
- **The kettle.** Nobody in the workshop can produce a drinkable coffee. The milk is a running jurisdictional dispute.
- **Jarrah's vape.** Appears in frame. Never commented on.
- **Two millimetres.** Sam's universal tolerance. It's real, it's the industry standard, and it will eventually be used against him.

## Trade authenticity — get this right or the whole thing dies

The single fastest way to lose a trade audience is a wrong detail. Use real material:

- **Board & finish:** HMR board, MDF, melamine, 2-pac polyurethane, veneer, edge banding, Laminex, Polytec, Formica.
- **Hardware:** Blum hinges and runners, soft-close, Tandembox, Hettich, Häfele, J-pull vs. handles.
- **Machines:** panel saw, CNC router (nesting), edgebander, spindle moulder, thicknesser, track saw, Domino, brad nailer, orbital sander.
- **Process, in order:** quote → 50% deposit → check measure → shop drawings → sign-off → cut & assemble → install day → scribing to walls that are out of square → stone template → benchtop install → splashback → kickboards & filler panels → defect list → final claim.
- **Business friction:** variations, progress claims, the designer who specced something impossible, the builder who didn't leave the right gap, "while you're here," the defect list, retention.
- **Trade politics:** the stone guys are a separate trade and always late. Bunnings is for homeowners, and buying trade stock there is a mild humiliation. A chippy is not a cabinet maker.
- **Geography:** workshops in Thomastown, Campbellfield, Bayswater, Braeside, Dandenong South. Clients in Brighton, Toorak, Hawthorn, Kew, Malvern, Northcote.

## Melbourne texture — the grievance fuel

Hook turns. Punt Road. The CityLink toll. Tradie parking permits and the inspector.
The 7am noise restriction. The Bunnings sausage sizzle and the onion-placement directive.
The flat white and the fact that only one place near the workshop can make one. Four
seasons in one day. AFL tribalism as a load-bearing social structure. The ute-versus-van
question. Merging on the Ring Road and the obligation to wave.

## Tone statement

Shot like a documentary crew turned up at a small business on a normal Tuesday and
nobody adjusted their behaviour. Straight performances, real noise, available light,
and no music inside the scenes. The only thing telling you it's a comedy is the four
bars of mandolin between them.
