# The 60-Second Format

Sixty seconds is not a short episode — it's a different mechanism. The half-hour show
converges four threads. At this length you can plant and fairly pay **two**. Everything
below follows from that.

---

## The beat clock: the Two-Thread Snap

| Time | Beat | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:02 | Title card on black, music sting | Tonal permission (see `03` — this is the only thing telling the audience it's comedy) |
| 0:02–0:16 | **SCENE A — THE RULE.** Sam states or defends the rule to a friendly (Kosta, Jarrah, Nic). **The seed is planted in the last line**, thrown away over an exit or an action. | Recruit the audience to his side, hide the bomb |
| 0:16–0:32 | **SCENE B — THE APPLICATION.** Sam applies the rule to a stranger who has, or will have, power over him. He wins the argument and loses the room. | Escalation |
| 0:32–0:50 | **SCENE C — THE COLLISION.** New location. The Scene B stranger turns out to be attached to the Scene A seed. | The trap closes |
| 0:50–0:57 | **THE SNAP.** One accusation. **One beat of silence.** Sam's defence, which is technically correct and completely irrelevant. | Payoff |
| 0:57–1:00 | **THE BUTTON.** One line — usually a callback that turns his own rule against him — then hard cut to black + sting. | Out |

## Hard constraints

- **Two threads. Never three.** Three is the short form's characteristic failure: you won't have time to plant the third fairly, and an unfair trap is worse than a simple one.
- **Three locations maximum.** One should be the van, unless the rule is self-evident enough to skip the theory beat.
- **Four speaking parts maximum.**
- **Runtime 58–62 seconds.**
- **Word count 135–165.** Count it — actually count it, don't estimate. Above 165 you have removed the silence, and the silence is the show.
- **The silence trade.** The band assumes one held pause. Each *additional* held beat you buy (a deciding shot, a closed door, a walk) costs roughly 12–15 words, so an episode with two or three of them lands legitimately around 110–125. Below ~100 you no longer have an episode, you have a gag.
- **The seed must be audible and skippable.** A viewer should hear it and not register it — put it at the end of a line, delivered while walking away, over the noise of an action.
- **The last line is Sam's, or it's about Sam while he says nothing.** Never end on someone else's joke.
- **The Consequence character (Voula) gets ≤12 seconds** and does not appear in most episodes.

## The Snap is the whole thing

Everything before it is machinery. The Snap has an exact three-part shape:

1. **The accusation** — short, flat, from someone with power. Understated (see the understatement scale in `04`). Never shouted.
2. **One beat of silence.** Hold it past comfortable. This is the single pause you're allowed to keep in the whole episode.
3. **Sam's defence** — technically correct, verifiable, and completely beside the point. He is not lying and not backing down. He is citing a standard.

Then the button, which should ideally **use his own unit of measure against him.** He
dies by his own tolerance.

## Shot grammar

The look is `03-production-craft.md` applied. Non-negotiables:

- **Two cameras, rolling simultaneously.** Camera A over Sam's shoulder onto his opponent; Camera B over the opponent's shoulder onto Sam. This geometry is 80% of the show's visual language and costs nothing but a second body. **Both must roll at once** or you cannot cut the pauses out and keep the sides matching.
- **Handheld, always.** Slight drift, small corrective reframes, the operator always a half-beat behind the conversation. Never locked off. Never a slider, never a gimbal.
- **One establishing wide per location, 1.5 seconds maximum.** Roller door, van, the plastic sheeting on the Brighton floor. Then straight into the geometry.
- **No new angle on the punchline.** Stay in the two-shot pair. Cutting to a fresh setup for the payoff tells the audience a joke is coming.
- **No reaction shot longer than 1.5 seconds. Cut on the flinch.**
- **Lenses 24–50mm equivalent.** Close enough to be uncomfortable. This is a show about people standing slightly too near each other.
- **Available light.** Practicals, roller-door daylight, fluorescent tubes. Blown-out windows are correct. Do not grade it into a look — it should read as a documentary crew who turned up on a Tuesday.
- **Sound:** location audio, real room tone. Dust extractor, compressor, Triple M, tram, reversing beeper. No sweetening, no SFX.

## Music

Per `03`: **no music inside scenes, ever.** Music exists only as the 2-second title
sting, a 1-second scene-transition sting, and the button hit. Use the original cue
commissioned to the brief in `03-production-craft.md` — **not "Frolic"**, which is
licensed and will get the episode taken down.

## Editing

- Cut all the air except the one pause after the accusation.
- Cut on overlap: enter the incoming line under the tail of the outgoing one.
- Hard cut to black on the button. **No fade.** No held reaction, no walk-away, no resolution.
- Total assembly should feel two clicks faster than comfortable, then stop dead.

## The 25-point QA checklist

Nothing ships until this passes. The numbering matters — check in order, since early
failures invalidate later checks.

### Story
1. Can you state Thread 1 and Thread 2 in one sentence each?
2. Was Thread 2 planted in Scene A and paid in Scene C?
3. **Is Sam technically correct about the rule?** (The most common failure. If no, rewrite the rule, not the scene.)
4. Would a stranger in a pub nod at the rule?
5. Is Sam punished for something *adjacent* to his actual rudeness, not identical to it?
6. Does the button use his own rule or his own unit of measure against him?
7. Does anyone state the theme out loud? (Cut it.)
8. Does any character explain a joke or a relationship? (Cut it.)
9. Is there a resolution? (There must not be one.)
10. On a rewatch, is the trap visible in Scene A? If not, it's unfair — plant it earlier and smaller.

### Voice
11. Does any character tell a joke with a setup and a punchline? (Cut.)
12. Is Voula's total screen time under 12 seconds, with no theory and no change of heart?
13. Does Bazza's advice actively make things worse, delivered as though obvious?
14. Does Sam ever look embarrassed? (He must not. Baffled and indignant only.)
15. Is every escalation *quieter* than the last, per the Melbourne register?
16. Read every line aloud. Cut any line an actor would resist saying.
17. Check against the understatement scale in `04` — has anyone described a catastrophe as a catastrophe?

### Continuity — this is what makes clips cut together
18. Wardrobe matches `05-series-bible.md` exactly: polo, Blundstones, pencil behind the right ear, safety glasses on forehead.
19. The van reads HENNING & OSTAS. The K is still peeling, and no better than last episode.
20. Locations dressed identically to previous episodes — same whiteboard, same kettle, same Pistone's table.
21. Prop tracking: where is the tape measure this episode, and on whose belt does it end up?
22. Names spelled exactly as in the bible. Fiona, Kosta, Voula, Bazza, Trev, Nic, Jarrah, Craig, Dean.
23. Trade detail verified: real hardware, real board, correct process order (`05`, trade authenticity).

### Technical
24. Runtime 58–62s; word count 135–165; ≤3 locations; ≤4 speaking parts.
25. No music under dialogue. Hard cut to black on the button. Both cameras rolled simultaneously on every two-hander.
